About the color and lighting model

One baseline per paint. RGB values were verified against the current Sherwin-Williams color pages on October 11, 2026. The house uses a fixed AI renovation image for each view, with its siding color removed: only neutral light/dark texture is retained before applying the selected paint. White trim, the natural wood door, the foundation and surroundings remain fixed.

Shared illustrative lighting. Neutral baseline has no added color cast or brightness adjustment. Warm daylight applies a modest warm tint and +0.3 stop adjustment; Cool shade applies a modest cool tint and −0.3 stop adjustment. Each setting uses exactly the same transformation for every paint and both comparison panes. These adjustments affect the siding only; they do not move shadows, change the weather, or model a particular time of day.

The shading field comes from the base image’s linear luminance, normalized to a brighter ordinary siding tone for each view. It is shared across all six colors, with no per-paint fitting or photo-sample transfer. This is a consistent display-color approximation, not a calibrated reflectance or physical lighting simulation. Masks are approximate around fine foliage and railing edges.

Real-house photos are references. The 22 previously measured patches from 21 photographs remain available below the preview. Browsing them changes only the reference image. Their lighting and camera processing no longer recolor your house. Studio Blue Green’s two references still carry their attribution warning; its house preview uses the manufacturer’s digital reference.

The renovation assumes horizontal lap siding throughout, existing white trim and gray foundation, and an illustrative natural wood door. Confirm finalists on actual painted siding at your house. Earlier AI images and generation notes are retained in the repository as prior studies.
